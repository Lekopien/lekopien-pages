The Arbiter’s Corner

Fuel for the Chessboard: How Diet, Sleep and Movement Help You Play Better Games

By Sarran Mahonye Otieno · October 2026 · 2 min read

This is the post where my two worlds collide. As an arbiter, I see players fade in the fifth hour of a game or the last round of a weekend event. As a dietitian, I can usually guess why.

Your brain is an endurance athlete

Thinking hard for hours is tiring. Your brain relies heavily on a steady supply of glucose, and stress hormones rise in tense games. Long tournament days, often several rounds back to back, are a test of stamina as much as calculation.

Before the round

  • Eat a balanced meal 2 to 3 hours before: some slow carbs (sweet potato, ugali, rice, whole-grain bread), some protein (eggs, beans, chicken) and vegetables.
  • Avoid a heavy, greasy plate right before you sit down. It can leave you sluggish for the opening.
  • Caffeine: a cup of tea or coffee can help focus, but don’t overdo it. Too much can make you jittery and impulsive, and that’s exactly when blunders happen.

During long games

  • Keep water at the board (if the regulations allow) and sip regularly. Even mild dehydration can affect concentration and mood.
  • Pack small snacks for games over three hours: a banana, groundnuts, dates or a few slices of bread. Check the event rules about eating in the playing hall.
  • Stand up and stretch during your opponent’s long thinks, following the event rules about leaving the board.

Between rounds

Eat something light with carbs and protein, refill your water, and get away from the board for a few minutes. Don’t spend the whole break replaying your loss in your head. Fresh air beats a fresh grudge.

The role of sleep and exercise

Sleep is when your brain consolidates what you’ve learned. Players who study late into the night before a tournament often play worse, not better. Regular exercise, even brisk walking, supports concentration and mood. Many top players train physically for exactly this reason.

What other games teach us

Whether it’s chess, draughts, bao, Scrabble or a long board-game night, the same habits apply: manage your energy, stay hydrated, and don’t make big decisions while tired or hungry. And always respect the rules and the people across the table. That’s what makes games worth playing.

Rules referenced here follow the FIDE Laws of Chess. Event regulations can change some details (for example, mobile phone rules or time controls), so always read the regulations of the tournament you are playing.

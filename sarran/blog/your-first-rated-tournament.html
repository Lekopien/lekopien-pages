Your First Rated Tournament: A Survival Guide from the Arbiter’s Table

By Sarran Mahonye Otieno · October 2026 · 2 min read

Every tournament, I meet a few players at their very first rated event. They’re usually excited, a little nervous, and not sure where to sit. This guide is for you.

Before the tournament

  • Register early with the organiser and check whether you need a FIDE ID. Your national federation can help you get one if the event is FIDE-rated.
  • Read the regulations: time control, default time (how late you can arrive before losing by forfeit), dress code, phone policy and tie-breaks.
  • Practise with a clock. If you have only played online, a physical digital clock feels very different. Get used to pressing it.
  • Practise writing moves in algebraic notation. In standard games you must record your moves.

On the day

  1. Arrive early and find the pairings board. It tells you your board number and whether you have White or Black.
  2. Set up and check the board: white square on your right, queen on her colour, and the clock set to the correct time control.
  3. Shake hands and wait for the arbiter to start the round. White’s clock is started when the round begins.
  4. Write first or move first? Under FIDE Laws you make your move on the board, then write it down. Writing your move before playing it is not allowed.

During the game

  • Keep quiet. No talking about your game with anyone, including your coach or friends.
  • If you need to leave the board, follow the event rules and leave your phone behind.
  • If something goes wrong, such as an illegal move by your opponent or a clock problem, pause the clock and raise your hand for the arbiter. Don’t argue across the board.
  • When the game ends, sign both scoresheets, make sure the result is correct, and report it as the regulations say.

Managing nerves

Nerves are normal. Breathe slowly before the first move, sit up straight, and focus on one move at a time. Bring water and a simple snack such as a banana or groundnuts; long rounds burn more mental energy than you’d expect.

After the tournament

Results are submitted for rating, and your first rating appears once you have played enough rated games. Win, lose or draw, keep your scoresheets. They are the best training material you’ll ever have, as I explain in How to Grow as a Player.

Rules referenced here follow the FIDE Laws of Chess. Event regulations can change some details (for example, mobile phone rules or time controls), so always read the regulations of the tournament you are playing.

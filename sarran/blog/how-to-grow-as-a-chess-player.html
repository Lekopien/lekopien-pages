How to Grow as a Player: A Training Plan That Actually Works

By Sarran Mahonye Otieno · October 2026 · 2 min read

From the arbiter’s table you see something interesting: players who improve quickly rarely have a secret opening. They have good habits. Here is the structure I recommend to club and school players who want to get noticeably stronger.

1. Tactics every day

Most games below master level are decided by tactics: forks, pins, skewers, discovered attacks and back-rank mates. Solve 15 to 30 minutes of puzzles a day. Choose puzzles slightly below your limit and solve them accurately, rather than racing through hard ones by guessing.

2. Learn the essential endgames

Endgames teach you how the pieces really work. Start with:

  • King and queen vs king, king and rook vs king (checkmate patterns)
  • King and pawn vs king, the idea of opposition and the “square of the pawn”
  • Basic rook endgames, including the Lucena and Philidor positions

Many players lose winning positions because they don’t know these. One hour a week here is gold.

3. Play slower games

Blitz is fun, but you improve most in games where you have time to think. Aim for at least one or two games a week with 15 minutes or more per player, online or over the board.

4. Analyse your own games, honestly

This is the habit that separates improving players from stuck ones. After each serious game:

  1. Go through it without an engine first. Write down where you think you went wrong and what you were thinking.
  2. Then check with an engine to find the moments that really mattered.
  3. Look for patterns: do you keep losing to the same opening? Running out of time? Missing your opponent’s threats?

5. Openings: principles before memorisation

Below about 1800, control the centre, develop your pieces, castle, and connect your rooks. Pick one opening for White and one defence each against 1.e4 and 1.d4, and learn the ideas rather than 20 moves of theory.

A sample week

DayFocus
Mon to Fri20 minutes of tactics
TuesdayOne endgame topic, 45 minutes
WednesdayOne slow game + analysis
SaturdayClub or tournament play
SundayAnalyse Saturday’s games, review an annotated master game

Don’t forget the player

Sleep, exercise and eating properly matter for your chess as much as for your body. Your brain is doing the work, so treat it like an athlete would. More on that in Fuel for the Chessboard.

Rules referenced here follow the FIDE Laws of Chess. Event regulations can change some details (for example, mobile phone rules or time controls), so always read the regulations of the tournament you are playing.

For caregivers

Caring for someone at home? Start here

Feeding a loved one who is unwell is hard work. These clinical tips help in the meantime, and a guidance session turns them into a plan for your person.

Keep a simple diary

Write down meals, drinks, a weekly weight, and any blood sugar or blood pressure readings. It makes every doctor or dietitian visit far more useful.

Small, rich, frequent meals

When appetite is poor, offer 5 to 6 small meals instead of 3 big ones. Enrich uji with milk, groundnut paste or an egg, and add avocado or oil to vegetables.

Offer drinks often

Older adults feel less thirsty. Offer water, milk, soup or tea every hour or two, and watch for dark urine, dizziness or confusion.

Watch the swallow

Coughing or choking while eating, a wet-sounding voice, or food left in the mouth can mean swallowing problems. Ask the doctor for a swallowing assessment before changing food textures yourself.

Match meals to medicines

Ask the doctor or pharmacist which medicines must be taken with food. With insulin or some diabetes tablets, never skip meals, and keep glucose sweets close for lows.

Look after yourself too

Caregivers skip meals and sleep. Eat regularly, accept help from family, and take breaks. You can’t pour from an empty cup.

Get medical help quickly if the person stops eating or drinking for more than a day, is losing weight without trying, chokes on food, becomes confused or very drowsy, or has very low or very high blood sugar readings. Call their doctor or go to the nearest hospital. For an emergency, don’t wait for a nutrition appointment.
